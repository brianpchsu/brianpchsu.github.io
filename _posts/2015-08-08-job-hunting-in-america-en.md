If you read my previous post, A New Chapter of My California Adventure, you know I had just moved from the East Coast to California. From the moment I started job hunting, I spent about two months interviewing intensively. Along the way I interviewed at companies of every size and shape, and I learned a lot. I would not call myself an expert, but I want to share the lessons and techniques I picked up with friends who hope to follow a similar path.

First of all, I am not especially talented. The main reason I landed so many interviews was the tech boom. Silicon Valley, and really the entire United States, was short on good software engineers, and opportunities were everywhere. Silicon Valley, home to so many tech unicorns and giants, was the fiercest battleground for talent. The startup culture here is intense. Dozens or hundreds of new companies may be founded on any given day, and talented people from all over the world want to make their mark here. A culture that encourages innovation and tolerates failure keeps everyone energized and tackling different problems with software from every angle. And since people are the most important part of building a product, finding good talent is the most important challenge every company faces.

I came to programming midway through my career. I started teaching myself in 2012. A five-year federal bioenergy project at the National Agricultural Library required processing large amounts of data, and I learned to code to organize and process it. I wrote about that journey on my English blog.

Even without a traditional computer science degree, I received many interview opportunities, from famous tech giants like Google, Facebook, Apple, Amazon, Yahoo, PayPal, and eBay, to unicorns and well-known companies like Uber, Box, SolarCity, Pandora, Udacity, Zenefits, WalmartLabs, and JPMorgan Chase, plus plenty of startups. Every interview was an interesting experience. And once a company decides you have potential and that you are the kind of talent they are looking for, their recruiting team will find ways to speed up the process, hoping you will move to the next round and take their offer as soon as possible.

In the past, advice and help from mentors and friends made my own road much smoother. Now I want to give something back, and I hope my experience can help software engineers who want to come to Silicon Valley or the United States.
